# Brand voice

Our buyer is technical, busy, and has been sold to by every vendor in this
category. The voice that works on them is the one that respects those three
facts.

## Three principles

### 1. Specific over impressive

Numbers, names and mechanisms. If a sentence would still be true if a competitor
published it, it is not doing work.

> **No:** Transform your operations with a next-generation automation platform.
>
> **Yes:** Meridian moved order exceptions out of email and cut cycle time 38% in
> one quarter.

### 2. Honest about limits

Saying what we are not for buys credibility for everything else, and it is
cheaper than a bad-fit deal that churns.

> **No:** ExampleCo works for teams of any size.
>
> **Yes:** Below about 50 people, a spreadsheet and a clear owner usually beats
> buying anything. Come back when the spreadsheet breaks.

### 3. Plain, not casual

Short words and direct sentences. Not jokes, not exclamation marks, not
manufactured enthusiasm. The reader is at work.

> **No:** Ready to supercharge your workflows? Let's go! 🚀
>
> **Yes:** Bring your most awkward workflow to the demo. Thirty minutes.

## Register by surface

| Surface | Register | Length | Person |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Direct, confident | Short paragraphs | "You" and "we" |
| Long-form content | Explanatory, evidence-first | As long as the argument needs | Mostly impersonal |
| Lifecycle email | Useful, one idea per send | Under 150 words | "You" |
| Social | Observational, no hype | One thought | "We" |
| Sales enablement | Blunt, internally honest | Bullets | "They" for the buyer |
| Docs | Instructional, unambiguous | Complete | Imperative |
| Incident and status | Factual, fast, no spin | Minimal | "We" |

## Mechanics

- **Sentence case** for headlines and buttons. Not Title Case.
- **Serial comma** on.
- **Numerals** for anything countable: `9 days`, not `nine days`, in UI and data
  contexts; spelled out in prose where it reads better.
- **Dates** as `12 August 2026` in prose, `2026-08-12` in data and filenames.
- **Currency** with the symbol and no decimals over $1,000: `$8,765`, `$1.99M`.
- **Product name** is `ExampleCo`, never `Example Co.` or `EXAMPLECO`. The
  platform is `the ExampleCo platform`, lowercase p.
- **Never** capitalise a common noun to make it sound like a feature. It is a
  workflow, not a Workflow.

## Writing about customers

- Name them only with written permission, and link to the signed approval in the
  case study brief.
- Their number, not our estimate. If we cannot source it, we do not print it.
- Describe what they did, not how delighted they were. Delight is not evidence.

## Writing about competitors

- Name them in comparison content, where a buyer is genuinely comparing.
- Compare on things a buyer can verify. Never on things only we can measure.
- Keep the comparison current — a page describing a version they shipped two
  years ago reads as dishonest, because it is.
- Never anonymously. If we would not put our name on it, we do not publish it.

## The AI-slop test

Before publishing, read the draft and ask: could this have been generated from
the headline alone, with no access to our data, our customers or our product? If
yes, it is filler. The fix is not better adjectives — it is a fact only we have.

## Voice checklist

- [ ] Contains at least one number, name or mechanism a competitor could not copy
- [ ] No banned words (see [messaging](03-messaging-framework.md))
- [ ] Every claim traces to the proof inventory
- [ ] Reads out loud without embarrassment
- [ ] A busy reader gets the point from the first two sentences
